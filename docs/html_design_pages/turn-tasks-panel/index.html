Turn tasks panel: 10 designs

Head chips that open a Tasks level in the Show bar; every subagent can open its session

The flow in every design: the turn head shows one chip per task (design 3 from the first round). Tapping a chip opens the Tasks level in the Show bar with that task in focus. Every subagent has an Open session → button that goes to its child session. The existing back bar brings you back.

Same moment in all ten: two Explore subagents (one finished), a code-reviewer subagent running its own pnpm test:unit, and a vitest watcher. Use the toggle top right for light and dark.

Ten Tasks panels

10
1

Table

↗

One row per task with fixed columns: name, kind, what it's doing now, time, actions. Fastest to scan and compare. The tapped chip's row is highlighted.

2

List and detail

↗

A short list on the left, the focused task's detail on the right: its prompt, model, cost, recent steps and its own tasks. The tapped chip picks the detail. Most information per tap; needs width, so a phone stacks the two.

3

Card grid

↗

Two columns of cards, each with its own mini activity strip, what it's doing now, and its buttons at the bottom. The friendliest to look at; uses the most height once there are more than four tasks.

4

Tree

↗

Tasks nest under whatever started them, with guide lines, so a subagent's own subagents and shells read as family. Actions show on hover (drawn on one row); the arrow is always visible on subagents.

5

Lanes

↗

The swimlanes from round one, living in the panel: each task is a bar on the turn's clock, with an arrow button to its session on the label. Best for 'what ran alongside what'; weakest at 'what is it doing right now'.

6

Accordion

↗

Compact rows that open one at a time. An open subagent shows its last few steps live, an open shell shows its output tail. The tapped chip opens its row. Keeps the panel short however many tasks there are.

7

Needs you first

↗

Sorted by what wants you: a subagent blocked on a permission comes first, with its answer buttons right there, then running, then done. Rows read as sentences. Drawn with the reviewer waiting on a Bash permission to show it.

8

Live feed

↗

One stream of everything the tasks are doing, newest first, each line tagged with its task. The head chips become filters: tap to show or hide a task. Tap a tag to open that subagent's session. Great for watching, poor for controlling.

9

Chips are tabs

↗

No separate list: the head chips turn into folder tabs and the tapped one joins a panel showing that task alone. One idea instead of two. Only one task at a time, and the chip row has to survive wrapping.

10

Grouped by kind

↗

Subagents and shells get their own sections because they need different things. Subagents show model, tokens and cost with Open session; shells show pid, output and a log link. Section totals answer 'what is this costing me'.

Decisions behind all ten

  1. Chip tap = open Tasks with that task focused. A second tap on the same chip closes it again, back to the level you were on.
  2. Open session → is on every subagent, finished ones too, because the child session outlives the task. It uses the same navigation as today's subagent card, so the existing back bar returns you to this turn.
  3. Shells get no session button, since they have no session. They get output and stop instead.
  4. Tasks only appears in the Show bar while the turn has tasks, and its count is the running ones.
