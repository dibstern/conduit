Stop: the turn vs background tasks

Today there is one Stop, and it kills everything: the turn plus every background task. The approved header pull-down adds a stop per task and "Stop all". The open question is what the composer's ■ does while background tasks are running.

The SDK catch

A options
Work today. Conduit's Stop closes the Claude process, which kills every task whatever else we declare. The pull-down's per-task stops still work.
B, C, D options
Need two changes. Conduit declares per-task stop to the SDK, and Stop goes back to interrupting the turn instead of closing the process. Then the SDK lets background work keep running when you stop the turn.
Unverified for shells. The SDK docs say an interrupt then spares "background agents and workflows". They don't mention background shell commands like pnpm dev. Before building any B, C or D option, a short test should confirm a background shell survives a turn-only stop.
My pick: B3. Stop ends only the turn, then a toast by the composer offers "Stop them" for a few seconds. You get your two-press flow without a double-tap killing your dev server, and the header pull-down covers everything after the toast fades. If you want exactly your idea, C2 is the safer form of it.

A · Stop keeps ending everything

No change to what Stop means. Per-task control lives only in the header pull-down.

B · Stop ends only the turn

Background work survives Stop. The options differ in where you go to stop it.

C · One button that escalates

Your idea and its variants: ■ stops the turn first, and the same control can then stop the tasks.

The choice is explicit in the composer itself.
