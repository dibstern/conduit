conduit · one session list for every project
mobile-first, with the settle lifecycle built in

The project switcher is gone. One list holds every session across every project, grouped by what it wants from you, filtered and scoped from the header, and parked with settle or snooze when it's done. This page is designed phone-first because conduit is a PWA you check from your pocket; the desktop layout is the same system with hover and keyboard added on top, never a different product.

1The system, one screen at a time

Status tiers are the outer grouping because that is what the product is for: the list answers what wants me right now before it answers anything else. Scope, filter and group all live in the header. Settle and snooze are the only things that collapse a row, and both are reachable three ways: a swipe, a sheet, and a keyboard shortcut.

2Scope, without the rail

The icon rail was a desktop habit. On a 340 px screen it costs 13% of the width permanently, carries no legible counts, labels projects with two-letter squares instead of names, and quietly rebuilds the container we just deleted. Seven replacements, judged on the same phone, and the decision is A with C living inside it.

Decided: A + C, one field, two ways in

The chip and the token are the same state rendered twice. Tap the chip and you get a picker; type project: and you get the identical token. Whichever you use, the result is a removable chip in the field and ?p=conduit in the URL, so the two can never disagree and nobody has to learn a syntax to use the product.

A+C · at restThe chip is the whole control

One 38 px row answers both "which sessions" questions. The chip is a word with a real target, not a glyph, and tapping it opens the picker with counts and live status.

Nothing here hints at syntax, because at rest nobody needs it.

Why the pair, and not either alone

  • A alone can't express "conduit, needs me, on feat/*, since Monday". You end up bolting a second filter language onto a picker.
  • C alone can't answer "which projects exist" at rest, and on touch it opens a keyboard to do the single most common thing.
  • Together the chip is the discoverable 80%, the token is the expressive 20%, and both write the same URL. Chips are the compiled form of tokens, which is the only way the two stay honest.
  • On desktop the same field takes / to focus and ⌘P to jump straight to the scope token.
One control, one state, two literacies.

Fallback

The least chrome of any option and the collapsed headers keep every project's urgency visible. But "show me only conduit for the next ten minutes" becomes a round trip through the group menu, and status tiers, the thing the product is actually for, get demoted to a second-level grouping.

Verdict: this is option A with the chip set to All. Keep it as a grouping, not as the answer to scope.
Nothing counts the sets that grow without bound. This is a data-model rule before it is a design one. A number next to Settled or Archived can only come from one of two places: a scan of every session that ever existed, or a client that is holding them all. Today the sidebar does the second — it subscribes to the whole session set and filters in the browser — and that is exactly what stops working once a person has been using conduit for a year. Drop those two counts and the subscription can be bounded to the live set, which is bounded by how much work one person can actually have in flight. The settled and archived shelves then load only when opened, a page at a time, and there is no aggregate to maintain or invalidate on every settle. The product argument points the same way: settled is where work goes to stop asking, the set only grows, it never falls on its own, and nobody acts differently on seeing 41 rather than 44. Want to know how many? Open the shelf and scroll, which is the same cost as caring. So the shelf header is a caret and a word, the scope picker’s Settled row has no tail, Include settled and Include archived are checkboxes with no total, and nothing anywhere sums either. Everything that is counted answers how much is waiting for me and is bounded by construction: Needs you 2, Running 2, Done unread 1, Snoozed 1 (it wakes up on its own, so the count is a promise). All 18 means eighteen sessions you can act on across four projects, not the forty-odd rows in the database. Chips, scope rows and group headers all count the same way, so the number you see is always the number of rows you get.
Net: option A ships. Option B is a config we can turn on for people with few projects, option C lives inside A's search field as the power syntax, option G is one entry in the group-by menu. The rail does not come back in any of them.

Touch rules the row obeys

  • 52 px row, comfortably over Apple’s 44 pt floor, and the whole row is the target rather than the title. Settled and snoozed rows sit at 40 px: a deliberate density trade in a collapsed shelf you visit rarely, still well clear of the 24 px WCAG minimum.
  • No control smaller than 34 px inside a row. That is why there are no per-row buttons on mobile at all: they would be 24 px or they would eat the title.
  • Swipe thresholds: 25% reveals and holds, 55% commits on release. Below 25% it springs back, so a scroll that drifts sideways never settles anything.
  • Every gesture has a sheet twin and a keyboard twin. Gesture-only actions are treated as unshipped.
  • Undo on every removal, 5 seconds, same toast on both platforms.

What the row refuses to do

  • No avatars, no message previews, no progress bars. The last assistant line is a hover card on desktop and a peek on long press, never a third line that shifts on every token.
  • No relative time that ticks. Times update on a 60 s interval and never re-sort the list.
  • No colour-only status. Each glyph has its own shape for the 8% who won't see the hue.
  • No row-level animation on arrival. New rows appear; they don't slide, because the list is something you read while it changes.

Accessibility

  • Row is a link with an aria-label that reads status first: “Needs approval, Fix auth token refresh, conduit, 2 minutes ago”.
  • One aria-live="polite" region for the whole list, announcing only tier-count changes, so a working session doesn't chatter.
  • Swipe actions are exposed as an action menu to VoiceOver and as <button>s to keyboard users.
  • Focus ring is the brand inset, identical to the open state, so keyboard position is never ambiguous.
  • prefers-reduced-motion drops the swipe spring and the spinner's dash rotation.

6Pills, buttons and the contrast rule

The old page had amber text on a near-white pill. That was not a palette choice, it was a bug: the drag badge's .verb rule painted a light background, and a more specific row rule then repainted only the text with a status colour. Two rules, each sensible alone, colliding into 1.6:1 contrast. The fix is a real system, not a one-off patch.

What it looked like

  • Light fill, light text, unreadable in sun and unreadable at 12 px.
  • Worse on a phone, where the screen is dimmer relative to ambient light and the pill is the thing your thumb aims at.
Every one of these fails WCAG AA for small text by a factor of two or more.

What replaces it, three tiers

Approve11.8:1 Reply9.4:1 ✓ Done Woke 3 selected
  • Loud — saturated fill, near-black ink (#0c0d10). Identical in both themes, because a fill is not a surface. Only for “this session is waiting on you”. At most two on screen.
  • Quiet — coloured text, no fill. Status you should notice but not act on.
  • Tint — surface fill, hairline border, secondary ink. Chips, counts, metadata, anything neutral.
A pill is loud, quiet or tint. There is no fourth option and no ad-hoc inline colour.

The rules that keep it from breaking again

  • Fill and ink are set together. A rule may never change one without the other. That single rule would have prevented the bug outright.
  • Status colours are ink-first. Amber, indigo, red and green are defined for use on a surface, so each theme owns its own value for them (§7). Using one as a background switches to the fill token and flips the ink to #0c0d10 automatically via the .loud modifier, in both themes.
  • No status colour is ever a light fill with its own hue as text.
  • Minimum 4.5:1 for anything under 16 px, checked in CI against the token pairs, not eyeballed.
  • Indigo was lightened from #7c7cff to #9b9bff so quiet indigo text clears AA on the dark surface. Light mode darkens the same token to #5344d4 instead: one name, two values, never a second class name.
Touch targets follow the same discipline. A pill that is also a button gets 44 pt of hit area even when it draws at 22 px, via padding or a pseudo-element. A pill that is only a label is never tappable, so it never invites a tap that does nothing.

The rule: a token is a surface or it is a fill

The four things light mode actually breaks

  • Shadows. rgba(0,0,0,.5) under a sheet reads as depth on near-black and as dirt on white. Two tokens, --sh and --shx, drop to about a seventh of the opacity and pick up a blue cast (rgba(19,26,37,.14)) so the shadow belongs to the same grey family as the borders.
  • Glass. The floating island and the floating button are rgba(24,25,29,.9) over a blur. In light they become rgba(255,255,255,.88). Same blur, same border token, opposite base — hard-coding either one is the bug.
  • Scrim. A modal scrim is not black at lower opacity; on light it is a cool grey at .34, because a true black wash makes the page under a sheet look switched off rather than backgrounded.
  • Deliberate inversions. The undo toast and the keyboard tooltip are meant to be the opposite of the page. They ride --inv / --inv-ink, which swap between themes rather than shifting. Miss this and the toast quietly becomes invisible in one of the two.
All four are the same failure: a colour written as a literal instead of a token.

How a person gets each one

  • Default is the OS. First load reads prefers-color-scheme, and the page tracks OS changes live until someone picks a side explicitly.
  • The choice is sticky and local. Picking light or dark writes one key and stops the OS from overriding it. Nothing is stored server-side, so a device can differ from another device, which is what people actually want between a phone in the sun and a desktop at night.
  • No flash. The theme is set from a tiny script in <head> before first paint, not by a component after hydration. A dark-mode user loading a light flash is a bug report every time.
  • color-scheme is set with the tokens, so scrollbars, form controls and the browser's own UI match without any extra CSS.
Why two and not seven. Custom themes looked free and were not: every one of them is a surface nobody tests, a contrast pair nobody checks, and a support question nobody can reproduce. Two themes means the contrast matrix is small enough to check in CI on every token pair, and every screenshot in this document is reviewable in both with one tap. Anyone who wants a third is really asking for a bug we cannot see.

Familiar is not the same as right. The drawer is the pattern for apps whose list is a menu; ours is the work.

What the risky five taught us

  • The glance is the common case. Most returns to the list are a question, not a navigation, which is why V6 and V9 feel good and why the header badge in V1 does most of their job for one hundredth of the cost.
  • Spatial beats alphabetical for small sets. V7 is genuinely better at 8 sessions and worse at 47, which is an argument for a grid view option, not a grid app.
  • Removing navigation beats decorating it. V10 is the only variant that deletes the trip entirely, which is the right instinct and the wrong time: it is a second mode on top of a list that does not exist yet.
